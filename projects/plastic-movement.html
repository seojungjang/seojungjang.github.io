Immersive Music Exhibition

Plastic Movement Chapter I

What happens when the performer is removed and the environment itself becomes the primary presence?

Context

Created under pandemic restrictions for one audience member at a time, the work removed both live performers and operators. Conceived as an immersive music exhibition, six compositions totaling 30 minutes played in an infinite loop through 16 individual speaker channels. Rather than following a fixed visual route, each visitor chose a path through sound, producing a different “sound map” through the space.

Question

What happens when the performer is removed and the environment itself becomes the primary presence?

Method

Lighting, fog, sound, and programmed air movement unfolded through timed control as a self-running environment. Rather than following a performer, light organized visibility, atmosphere, transitions, and the visitor’s attention while the multi-channel sound field guided movement through the space.

What this opened

Removing the performer changed the function of lighting for me. Light was no longer supporting a body at the center of the event; together with sound, haze, and spatial timing, it became part of the event’s primary presence. This shifted my attention toward how environments can organize perception and shape interpretation.

Study 01

Building the environment

The suspended translucent structures, haze, distributed light sources, and 16-channel sound field turned the room itself into the material of the encounter. Six compositions, Breath, Hallucination, Bridle, Oblivion, Extinction, and Emptiness, circulated in an infinite loop while one visitor at a time moved through the installation.

Spatial elements

  1. One audience member at a time
  2. Six compositions / 30-minute loop
  3. 16 individual speaker channels
  4. ETC Eos + QLab timed control, without a live operator
  5. Light, haze, and translucent surfaces shaping a changing path
Study 02

Changing states

The same physical environment could read as materially and emotionally different through changes in color, direction, intensity, and haze. The comparison became an early way of thinking about environment as an active perceptual system.

Reflection

What this changed

Without a performer to anchor attention, the designed environment became the event itself. That experience moved my practice from lighting a subject toward designing the perceptual conditions through which an encounter unfolds.
