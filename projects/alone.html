Study 06

Alone

How can lighting shift visual emphasis among the live body, AI-generated text and imagery, and the surrounding environment?

Project

Presented at the 26th Seoul International Dance Festival (SIDance) in 2023, Alone brought together contemporary dance, lighting, projection, and AI-generated text and imagery. A custom GPT-4-based system developed for the performance allowed the choreographer to enter a starting sentence and specify how many sentences should follow, generating a textual continuation. AI-generated images were also incorporated into the projection. The final choreographic, visual, and theatrical decisions remained with the human collaborators.

My question

How can lighting shift visual emphasis among the live body, AI-generated text and imagery, and the surrounding environment?

My approach

I designed lighting cues, chose color temperatures for the performer, and timed transitions in relation to projected text and imagery. I decided when to isolate the body with light and when to place it within a shared visual field.

What this opened

A question about the relationship among body, projection, light, and timing: what changes when a computational system is given bounded authority to select among human-authored lighting states?

Reflection

What this changed

This work shifted my interest from AI-generated material toward the relationships among body, projection, light, and timing, and toward who or what determines those relationships.

Next question

Alone used AI-generated text and imagery as creative material, while artistic decisions remained with the human collaborators. It led me to ask what might change if a computational system were given bounded authority to select among human-authored lighting states. How would that selection change the timing and visibility of a body in relation to projected imagery?

Follow-up study in preparation: comparing the original human-operated cue sequence with bounded alternative selections for the same excerpt.
